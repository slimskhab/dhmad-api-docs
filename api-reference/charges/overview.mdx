title: Charges
description: Collect a payment for your own business from whoever opens the link

A **charge** collects money for **your** business — selling credits, top-ups, subscriptions, or anything else where you are the merchant. It is the DHMAD equivalent of a payment link.

Use a charge instead of an [escrow](/api-reference/escrows/overview) when you are the merchant: there is no delivery step and no contract. The payment is held for 72 hours, during which the payer can open a dispute, and then released to your wallet.

## Charge vs escrow

| | Charge | Escrow |
|---|---|---|
| Seller | Your business account | Your user, or your business |
| Buyer | Unknown until someone pays | Known at creation (`buyerEmail`) |
| Checkout URL | Public — anyone with the link can pay | Bound to the buyer's email |
| After payment | Completes immediately; funds held 72h, then released to your wallet | Deliver, then buyer release (or auto-release after `completionDeadlineDays`) |
| Refunds | `POST /v1/charges/:id/refunds` (full or partial); `POST /v1/charges/:id/dispute/accept` for a disputed charge | Seller refund via checkout session `refund_funds` |
| API | `POST /v1/charges`, `GET /v1/charges/:id`, `GET /v1/charges/:id/refunds` | `POST /v1/escrows`, `GET /v1/escrows/:id` |
| Escrow endpoints | **404** on GET; **409** `CHARGE_NOT_AN_ESCROW` on deliver / reject / update / sessions / contract | Charge ids are hidden from list and get |

## How it works

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create the charge">
    `POST /api/v1/charges` with the amount, a title, your own `merchantReference`, and a `redirectUrl`. You get back a checkout `url`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the payer to the URL">
    No DHMAD account is needed. The payer enters an email for the receipt and pays by card, PayPal, or bank transfer. It does not have to be the person who will be credited on your side.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Credit your user from the webhook">
    Subscribe to **`charge.status.updated`**. The payload includes `merchantReference` and `receiptEmail`. Look up your own record from `merchantReference` and credit it. Ignore `receiptEmail` for anything except receipts. `escrow.status.updated` may still fire for the same payment — treat `charge.status.updated` as the charge event.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Beneficiary vs payer

The person who benefits from a charge and the person who pays it are separate:

- The **beneficiary** lives entirely on your side. DHMAD only sees your opaque `merchantReference`, so a manager can pay for an employee's credits without either of them sharing an account.
- The **payer** is whoever opens the URL. DHMAD collects their email at checkout for the receipt and reports it back as `receiptEmail`.

Because of this, always credit from `merchantReference`, never from `receiptEmail`.

## Idempotency

`merchantReference` is unique per developer and acts as the idempotency key. Creating a charge again with the same reference returns the original charge (with a fresh checkout URL if the previous one expired) instead of charging twice. That makes it safe to retry a failed request, and it is also how you resume an abandoned payment.

## Requirements

- Your developer account must have a business account. Funds settle into that business's DHMAD wallet.
- In live mode, the business must pass KYB before it can take charges.
- `redirectUrl` must match one of the allowed redirect URLs in your [Developer Dashboard](https://developer.dhmad.tn/dashboard).

## Not an escrow

A charge is a separate product. Do not create `mode: "instant"` on `POST /v1/escrows` — that is rejected. Do not send a charge id to escrow lifecycle endpoints:

- [Get Escrow](/api-reference/escrows/get-escrow) and [List Escrows](/api-reference/escrows/list-escrows) hide charges (GET returns **404**).
- Deliver, reject-delivery, update, contract, progressive release, and [checkout sessions](/api-reference/checkout-sessions/create-session) on a charge id return **409** `CHARGE_NOT_AN_ESCROW`.
- Charges do not use `completionDeadlineDays`. Payment completes in one step; funds settle to your wallet after the 72-hour hold.

## Refunds

Refund a charge in full or in part with [Refund Charge](/api-reference/charges/create-refund). During the 72-hour hold the refund comes out of the held payment and DHMAD's fee on the refunded part is waived; after release it comes out of your available balance and DHMAD keeps its fee. Follow refunds with the `charge.refunded` and `charge.refund.updated` webhooks, and list them with [List Refunds](/api-reference/charges/list-refunds).

## Disputes

During the 72-hour hold the payer can open a dispute with DHMAD. The release then waits (`fundsStatus: "disputed"`), you receive the `charge.dispute.opened` webhook, and [Get Charge](/api-reference/charges/get-charge) shows who opened it and why in `dispute`. Refunds return **409** `CHARGE_DISPUTED` while it is open. Settle it yourself with [Accept Dispute](/api-reference/charges/accept-dispute), which refunds the payer in full, or let DHMAD support review it and either refund the payer or release the payment to you.

## Chargebacks

When a payer disputes a PayPal or card payment with PayPal or their bank, DHMAD holds back the disputed amount — by blocking the release during the hold, or from your available balance after it — and opens an urgent support ticket asking for your evidence. If your balance does not cover it, money that reaches your balance later is held too, until the disputed amount is covered or the case is decided; all of it comes back if you win. If PayPal sides with the payer, the amount is taken back and PayPal's chargeback fee is passed on to you. Follow cases with the `charge.chargeback.opened` and `charge.chargeback.resolved` [webhooks](/guides/webhooks).
